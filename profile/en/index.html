Now

Founded Wanderlust in 2023 and, after four pivots, moved into generative AI in June 2024. Until July 2025 we also built an enterprise SaaS in the ETL space, but ACV fell short of plan, so we closed it and consolidated into consulting and custom development. We work mainly with large enterprises, covering everything from process assessment and PoC through to full build and company-wide rollout.

The business is stable, but the requirements here are well defined and the shape of the answer is largely known. I want to work on something less certain and longer in horizon, which is why I am pursuing an M&A for Wanderlust.

Next

My working view is a single one: what ultimately caps AI is not semiconductors but power, cooling and land. Compute demand will keep growing, but the generation, transmission, cooling and sites that carry it take years to procure and build, and do not scale the way software does. As long as the constraint sits on the physical side, that is where value will pool.

Areas under exploration
01
Power & data centers
Securing power, cooling and sites. The most direct place AI demand lands, and the most constrained.
02
Semiconductors
The surrounding layers: equipment, materials and back-end processes. Japan holds a number of world-class makers in precision machinery and materials, and that strength still counts.
03
Space
SpaceX and Blue Origin have largely settled the question of transport. What interests me is the infrastructure that has to come after it.
04
Robotics
Automation that reaches into the physical workplace as the labor force shrinks. The implementation side of AI.
05
Shipping
A field I have followed for years. Fleets and routes decide the competition, and the barrier to entry is built out of capital and time.
06
Shipbuilding
Capacity is concentrated in a handful of countries and supply is not keeping up with demand. I am interested in what can still be done with the technology and facilities Japan retains.
How I choose the field
  • Demand that can be read on a fifty-year horizon
  • High barriers to entry, and a mission that is genuinely hard
  • Something software alone cannot deliver
  • A contribution to Japan’s GDP
  • An area where decoupling from China places Japan favorably within the US–Japan geopolitical ecosystem
